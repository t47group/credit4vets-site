How to get your credit reports for free (and why you should)

Credit4Vets· September 9, 2026 ·3 min read

Before anyone can help you improve your credit, you need to know what is actually on your reports. A surprising number of people have never looked — and among those who do look, errors are common.

The good news: getting your reports costs nothing, and you can do it today.

One website, three reports

The only federally authorized source is AnnualCreditReport.com.

That site gives you reports from all three nationwide credit bureaus — Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion. Since October 2023, the bureaus have made free weekly reports permanently available through it, according to the Federal Trade Commission.

Weekly. Not once a year, which is what most people still believe.

Watch out for the sites that charge

Search "free credit report" and you will find many results that are not the official site. Some offer a free report as the entry point to a paid monthly subscription. Others charge a fee outright.

Three things to check before entering any information:

  • The address is exactly annualcreditreport.com
  • You are never asked for a credit card number
  • Nobody is enrolling you in a monthly service

If any of those is off, close the tab. The real site asks for identifying information to verify who you are — name, address, Social Security number, date of birth — and some security questions from your credit history. It does not ask for payment.

Report is not the same as score

This confuses almost everyone, so it is worth being clear.

Your credit report is the record: accounts, balances, payment history, collections, public records, and who has requested your file. Free weekly from the official site.

Your credit score is a number calculated from that record. There are many different scoring models and lenders use different ones. Free scores are widely available from card issuers and banks, but a score by itself does not tell you what to fix.

The report is where the useful information lives. If you are trying to improve your credit, start there.

What to look for on each report

Go through all three, because information is not always identical across bureaus.

Accounts you do not recognize. These may be identity theft, a mixed file with someone of a similar name, or an account listed under a former name.

Wrong payment history. A late payment marked when you paid on time. Payment history carries substantial weight, so these matter.

Balances that are wrong or outdated. Especially accounts you have paid off that still show a balance.

Debts listed twice. A collection account sometimes appears both from the original creditor and the collection agency, making one debt look like two.

Old items that should have aged off. Most negative information can only be reported for a limited period. Items that have outlived that window should not still be there.

Addresses and employers you have never had. Often harmless, sometimes the first sign that your file has been mixed with someone else's.

Why this matters more after service

Military life produces specific patterns that show up on credit reports.

Frequent moves mean many addresses on file, which raises the chance of a mixed file. Deployments can mean bills handled by someone else, or not handled at all. A permanent change of station can leave a final utility bill at an old address that quietly went to collections. And service members who have not carried much consumer credit can have a thin file — not bad credit, just not much history — which lenders sometimes treat as if it were bad.

None of that is a personal failing. It is the paperwork trail of a particular kind of life, and it is fixable.

Write down what you find

Before you do anything else, get all three reports and note anything that looks wrong: which bureau, which account, and what specifically is incorrect.

That list is the starting point for everything that follows — and if you bring it to us, it is exactly what our counselors need to help you decide what to do next.

Credit4Vets works with veterans on credit, financing, housing, and business support at no cost. If you would like help reading your reports or deciding what to dispute, reach out.
