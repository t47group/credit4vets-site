Medical bills are one of the most common reasons people end up in collections, and they rarely arrive at a good time. For veterans, there is an extra layer: VA copays, community care providers, private insurance, and sometimes a bill that should never have been sent to you at all.

The rules about whether medical debt can appear on your credit report have also changed more than once in the last few years, and a lot of what is circulating online is out of date. Here is where things stand.

## What the credit bureaus changed

In 2022, the three nationwide credit bureaus — Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion — announced voluntary changes to how they handle medical collections. According to a Congressional Research Service overview, those changes mean:

- **Paid medical collection debt is excluded** from credit reports.
- **Medical debt in collections for less than a year is excluded**, giving people time to sort out insurance and billing problems.
- **Medical collections under $500 are excluded.**

These were changes the bureaus made themselves, and they are still the main protection most people have.

## What happened to the federal rule

You may have heard that medical debt was being removed from credit reports entirely. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau did finalize a rule in early 2025 that would have removed most medical debt from credit reports and limited lenders from using it.

That rule never took effect. It was challenged in court, and in July 2025 a federal judge vacated it, finding that it went beyond the CFPB's authority. So medical collections of $500 or more that are at least a year old and unpaid can still appear on your reports.

## How VA debt is reported

VA changed its own policy in 2022. According to VA, it will not report debt to the credit reporting agencies until all available collection efforts have been exhausted. VA also said it will not report debt owed by veterans who are rated catastrophically disabled and entitled to cost-free health care because of low income. Debt involving fraud, misrepresentation, or bad faith can still be reported.

That does not mean VA debt goes away if you ignore it. VA's debt management page warns that unpaid or unresolved debts can be reported to credit agencies, and that by law, delinquent debts are referred to the U.S. Treasury, which can take money from tax refunds and other federal payments. If you have a VA copay bill you cannot afford, ask about options early.

**For VA copay questions,** VA lists the Health Resource Center at 866-400-1238. For current bills, VA lists three options: a waiver, a compromise (a smaller one-time payment), or a repayment plan. If your income has dropped, you can also request a hardship determination, which may exempt you from VA copays for the rest of the calendar year (pharmacy copays are not covered). VA says to act within 30 days of receiving your bill to avoid late charges, interest, and fees.

## Community care bills

If VA authorized you to see a community provider and that provider sends you a bill, do not just pay it. VA's community care customer service guide says, "You should never pay a community provider directly."

Instead, contact the **National VA Community Care Contact Center at 877-881-7618**. Keep the bill, your referral or authorization information, and notes of every call.

This matters for your credit, because a provider who is not paid may eventually send the account to a collection agency. Catching a billing mix-up early is much easier than cleaning up a collection later.

## An Arizona note

Arizona voters passed Proposition 209 in 2022. According to the Arizona courts' self-service center, it caps interest on medical debt at the lesser of 3% or the one-year Treasury rate for the week before you were first billed. It also increased several of the protections for property and wages when a creditor wins a judgment.

If you are being charged a high interest rate on an old medical bill in Arizona, it is worth asking a legal aid office or attorney whether that cap applies to you.

## What to check on your own reports

Pull all three of your reports for free at [AnnualCreditReport.com](https://www.annualcreditreport.com). Then look for:

- **Paid medical collections** that are still showing
- **Medical collections under $500**
- **Medical collections less than a year old**
- **Bills that insurance, TRICARE, or VA should have covered**
- **Accounts you do not recognize at all**, which can be a sign of medical identity theft

If something is wrong, you can dispute it directly with each bureau that shows it. Disputes are free. Include copies of any proof you have — an explanation of benefits, a paid receipt, or a VA authorization.

## If a collector contacts you about a medical bill

Before you pay anything, ask for the details in writing. Debt collectors are generally required to send a validation notice explaining the debt, who the original creditor is, and how to dispute it.

Then check whether the bill should have gone to insurance, TRICARE, or VA in the first place. Many medical collections turn out to be billing errors rather than debts the patient truly owes.

## Before you put a medical bill on a credit card

When a bill is large, it can be tempting to put it on a credit card or take a "medical credit card" offered at the front desk. The CFPB warns that the medical debt credit reporting protections do not apply to unpaid medical bills on a medical credit card or financing plan — so missed payments can show up on your report like any other card. Many of these products also use deferred interest: if the balance is not paid in full by the end of the promotion, or a payment is missed, interest can be charged on the full amount from the start.

Talk to the provider's billing office first. Many offer payment plans or financial assistance, and asking costs nothing.

## A note on this guide

Credit reporting rules, VA policies, and state laws change, and how they apply depends on your situation. This is general information, not legal or financial advice. Confirm the details with VA, the credit bureaus, or a legal aid office.
