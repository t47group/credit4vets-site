Housing instability affects veterans across every income level and background, and it frequently follows an event rather than a pattern — a job loss, a medical bill, a divorce, a lease that ended badly.

If that is where you are right now, this post is meant to be useful immediately.

## Start with one phone call

**National Call Center for Homeless Veterans: 877-424-3838.**

It is free, confidential, and available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. A trained VA staff member asks a few questions about your situation and connects you with resources near you.

You do not have to be currently homeless to call. The center serves veterans who are at risk as well, and reaching out earlier gives you far more options than waiting.

**You also do not have to be the veteran.** Family members, friends, and service providers can call on someone's behalf. If you are worried about a veteran you know, that number works for you too.

## The main programs

**HUD-VASH** combines a Housing Choice Voucher — rental assistance from HUD — with case management and clinical services from the VA. It is aimed at veterans experiencing homelessness who need ongoing support alongside housing.

**SSVF (Supportive Services for Veteran Families)** works differently. It funds community organizations to provide rapid re-housing and homelessness prevention, including help with rent arrears, deposits, and utility costs. SSVF is often the right fit when the need is short-term and specific — a veteran with income who has fallen behind and needs help catching up rather than long-term support.

**Grant and Per Diem** funds transitional housing through community providers, generally as a step toward permanent housing.

Eligibility differs by program, and the call center exists precisely so you do not have to work out which one fits before you ask for help.

## Bring what you have

It helps to have documents ready, but **do not delay calling because you cannot find them.** VA staff work with veterans who have lost their paperwork constantly, and there are ways to verify service.

If you can gather them, useful items include:

- DD-214 or other discharge documentation
- Photo identification
- Social Security card
- Income documentation, if you have income
- Any eviction notice or court paperwork, if applicable

## If you are facing eviction

Timing matters enormously here, because the options available before a court judgment are much broader than after one.

Call the number above, and also look into local legal aid. Many areas have organizations providing free legal representation in eviction cases, and some have programs specifically for veterans. Tenant rights and timelines vary significantly by state.

If you have received court paperwork, do not ignore it. Missing a court date usually produces an automatic judgment against you.

## Housing and credit are connected

Housing instability damages credit, and damaged credit makes housing harder to get. Evictions can appear in public records and tenant screening reports. Utility accounts go to collections. That cycle is one of the more common reasons veterans come to us.

Breaking it usually means dealing with both at once — stabilizing housing while working on what is reportable and correctable in the credit file. Neither problem fully resolves while the other is active.
