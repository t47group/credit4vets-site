Tax season does not officially start until the new year, but the easiest returns are usually the ones that were organized ahead of time. A few small tasks now — and a couple of dates on the calendar for January — can save you a missing form, a delayed refund, or a fraudulent return filed in your name.

Here is a short plan for the weeks between now and filing.

## 1. Make a tax folder today

Pick one place, paper or digital, where every tax document goes as it arrives. Start it now with:

- Last year's tax return
- Photo ID and Social Security cards or numbers for everyone on the return
- Your bank's routing and account number, for direct deposit of any refund
- Receipts for any charitable donations made this year

Then, as forms arrive in January and February, they go straight into the folder. No searching in March.

## 2. Know which forms to expect

Depending on your situation, you may receive:

- **Form W-2** from each employer you worked for during the year
- **Form 1099-R** for military retired pay, a pension, or retirement account distributions, including the TSP
- **Form SSA-1099** if you receive Social Security benefits
- **Forms 1099-INT or 1099-DIV** for bank interest or investment income
- **Forms 1099-NEC or 1099-K** if you did freelance or side work, or sold goods through a payment platform

Write down every place you got income from this year. That list is how you will know if a form is missing.

**For military retirees:** DFAS posts tax statements to **myPay**, and online copies are often available before mailed ones. For last tax year, DFAS posted retiree and annuitant 1099-Rs to myPay on December 17, 2025, and mailed paper copies by January 31, 2026. Check DFAS for this year's schedule. DFAS also recommends typing the myPay address directly into your browser rather than clicking links, to avoid spoofed sites.

## 3. If a form does not arrive

According to the IRS:

1. **Contact the employer or payer first** and ask for the missing or corrected form.
2. **If you still do not have it by the end of February**, call the IRS at 800-829-1040. Have your information and the employer's or payer's name, address, and, if you have it, their employer identification number ready.
3. The IRS will contact the employer, and send you **Form 4852**, which you can use to file using your best estimate if the form never shows up.
4. If the real form arrives later and the numbers are different, you may need to file an amended return.

## 4. Get an IRS Identity Protection PIN in January

An **Identity Protection PIN (IP PIN)** is a six-digit number that prevents someone else from filing a tax return using your Social Security number. Veterans, whose Social Security numbers have passed through a lot of hands over the years, have good reason to get one.

According to the IRS:

- **Anyone with a Social Security number or ITIN** who can verify their identity can get one. Parents and guardians can request one for dependents.
- **The fastest way** is through your IRS online account.
- **The online tool is available from mid-January through mid-November**, so if you missed it this year, put a reminder on your calendar for mid-January.
- **A new IP PIN is issued every calendar year.** If you get yours online, you retrieve the new one from your account each year.
- **The IRS never asks for your IP PIN** by phone, email, or text. Only you, your tax preparer when you are ready to file, and the IRS should know it.

Alternatives exist if you cannot use the online account, including an in-person appointment at an IRS Taxpayer Assistance Center.

## 5. Know what is not taxable

According to IRS Publication 525, you should not include in income any veterans' benefits paid under any law, regulation, or administrative practice administered by VA. The IRS lists examples including:

- Education, training, and subsistence allowances
- Disability compensation and pension payments for disabilities, paid to veterans or their families
- Grants for homes designed for wheelchair living
- Grants for motor vehicles for veterans who lost their sight or the use of their limbs
- Veterans' insurance proceeds and dividends

Military retired pay based on years of service, on the other hand, is generally taxable. If you receive both, or your VA rating changed with a retroactive effective date, ask a tax professional or VITA volunteer how it affects your return. Publication 525 has a section on retroactive VA determinations.

## 6. Watch for tax scams

Tax season is also impostor season. Remember:

- No real government agency, including the IRS, will tell you to pay with a gift card, the FTC says.
- The IRS does not ask for your IP PIN by phone, email, or text.
- If you get a call or message claiming to be the IRS and something feels off, hang up and contact the IRS directly using information from IRS.gov.

Report impostor scams to the FTC at [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/).

## 7. Book free tax help early

Free tax preparation through VITA, TCE, and MilTax is available to many veterans and military families. We covered who qualifies in our guide to [free tax preparation for veterans](/blog/free-tax-help-for-veterans/). Appointments fill up, so plan to book one as soon as sites open in the new year.

## 8. Decide now what the refund is for

If you expect a refund, decide before it arrives what it will do — pay down a high-rate balance, build an emergency fund, or cover a known expense coming up next year. A plan made in December is much easier to follow than one made the day the money lands.

## A note on this guide

Tax rules depend on your situation and can change from year to year. This is general information, not tax advice. Confirm the details with the IRS, DFAS, or a qualified tax professional or VITA volunteer.
