Many veterans carry federal student loans from before, during, or after their service. For some of them, there is a federal benefit that can wipe those loans out entirely, and it goes unused more often than it should.

It is called a **Total and Permanent Disability (TPD) discharge**, and VA disability determinations are one of the ways to qualify.

## Who qualifies through VA

According to Federal Student Aid at the U.S. Department of Education, you qualify for a TPD discharge based on a VA determination if either of these is true:

- **VA has rated a service-connected disability (or combination of disabilities) as 100% disabling**, or
- **VA has determined you are totally disabled based on an individual unemployability (TDIU) rating**

A combined rating below 100% without individual unemployability does not qualify through the VA route. There are other routes to TPD discharge — through Social Security disability determinations or certification from a medical professional — with their own rules.

## How the VA match works

You may not have to start the process yourself. Federal Student Aid says the Department of Education works with VA every quarter to identify borrowers who qualify. If you are identified, you will receive a letter explaining your discharge and any next steps.

Federal Student Aid also notes you may not get that letter right away after VA makes its determination. If you already have a qualifying rating and have not heard anything, you do not have to wait.

## How to apply if you did not get a letter

Federal Student Aid lists these options:

1. **Apply online** through the TPD Discharge Application on [StudentAid.gov](https://studentaid.gov/), signing in to your account. You will upload documentation from VA showing when your disability determination was awarded. This is the method Federal Student Aid recommends.
2. **Print the prefilled application**, sign it, and upload it using the Document Upload Tool, choosing "Total and Permanent Disability (TPD) Discharge" as the type.
3. **Download a blank application** and mail it with your documents to U.S. Department of Education, P.O. Box 300010, Greenville, TX 75403, or fax it to 540-212-2415.

A caregiver or representative can apply on your behalf by completing an Applicant Representative Designation form. You can track your application under "My Activity" in your StudentAid.gov account.

**What VA documentation to use:** a VA letter that shows your 100% service-connected rating or your individual unemployability determination, and the date it took effect. You can usually download a benefit summary letter from VA.gov.

## Taxes on a discharged loan

Forgiven debt can sometimes count as taxable income. For TPD discharges, federal law has excluded them from income for several years, and the 2025 tax law known as H.R. 1 made that exclusion permanent for discharges after December 31, 2025, according to the National Association of Tax Professionals. The exclusion covers both federal and private student loans discharged because of death or total and permanent disability, and the borrower's Social Security number must be included on the tax return for the year of discharge.

**State taxes may be different.** Some states do not follow federal rules on discharged debt. Check with your state's tax agency or a tax professional before you file.

## What about private student loans?

A TPD discharge from the Department of Education applies to federal student loans. Private student loans are a separate matter. Some private lenders offer their own disability discharge, but terms vary. Contact your private lender directly and ask what it offers.

## What it means for your credit and your budget

When a loan is discharged, the balance goes to zero, and the loan should be reported that way on your credit report. A few weeks after you get confirmation:

- **Pull your credit reports** at [AnnualCreditReport.com](https://www.annualcreditreport.com) and make sure each discharged loan shows a zero balance.
- **Dispute any loan** that still shows a balance or a late payment that should not be there.
- **Check your bank account** for any automatic payments that are still being drafted, and stop them if the loan has been discharged.

Then decide where your former student loan payment should go. For many veterans it is a meaningful amount each month. Directing it toward an emergency fund or another debt, on purpose, keeps it from simply disappearing into everyday spending.

## Do not pay anyone to do this for you

The TPD application is free through StudentAid.gov. The Federal Trade Commission is direct about student loan help in general: you do not have to pay for help managing your student loans, never pay an upfront fee, and never share your FSA ID.

Be especially wary of anyone who contacts you claiming they can "speed up" a disability discharge or "guarantee" forgiveness for a fee. Scammers use official-looking seals and logos. If you want help, use StudentAid.gov, your loan servicer, or a free counselor.

Report student loan scams to the FTC at [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/).

## VA debt is not student loan debt

One point that confuses people: VA notes that federal student loans are not VA debt, even if you used them alongside the GI Bill. A VA education overpayment is handled by VA. Federal student loans are handled by the Department of Education and your servicer.

## A note on this guide

Eligibility rules, application steps, and tax treatment can change, and they depend on your situation. This is general information, not legal or tax advice. Confirm your eligibility with Federal Student Aid and your tax situation with the IRS, your state tax agency, or a tax professional.
