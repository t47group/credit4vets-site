Every fall, a lot of veterans and military families wait on one number: the cost-of-living adjustment, or COLA. It affects Social Security, and through it, VA disability compensation and military retired pay.

In October, the Social Security Administration announced that number for 2027. Here is what it is, how it reaches the benefits veterans depend on, and how to work it into your budget without getting caught by the scams that always show up alongside it.

## The number: 3.6%

According to the Social Security Administration, Social Security and Supplemental Security Income (SSI) benefits will increase **3.6%** for 2027.

SSA says:

- **Social Security** beneficiaries will see the higher payments starting in **January 2027**.
- **SSI** recipients will see increased payments beginning **December 31, 2026**.
- The COLA is tied to the Consumer Price Index for Urban Wage Earners and Clerical Workers (CPI-W), calculated by the Bureau of Labor Statistics.

## When you will see your notice

If you have a **my Social Security** account, SSA says you can view your COLA notice online starting in **late November**, up to three weeks before paper notices arrive. To see it online, SSA says you need to create an account and opt in to paperless notices by **November 18**. Otherwise, your notice arrives by mail in December.

If you are enrolled in Medicare, SSA says your 2027 Medicare premium will also be visible in the my Social Security Message Center starting in late November. That matters, because a change in your premium can change how much of the increase you actually take home.

## VA disability compensation

VA disability compensation does not adjust automatically. Each year, Congress has to pass a law applying a cost-of-living increase to compensation and to Dependency and Indemnity Compensation (DIC) for survivors.

Bills introduced in 2026, including the Veterans' Compensation Cost-of-Living Adjustment Act of 2026, would set the increase at the same percentage as the Social Security increase, effective **December 1, 2026**. VA's rates page notes that compensation increases match the percentage of Social Security's COLA; the current rates on that page took effect December 1, 2025.

Until the new 2027 rates are posted, do not count on an exact figure. VA publishes official compensation rate tables on VA.gov once they are in place, and those are the numbers to use in your budget.

## Military retired pay and SBP

Military retired pay and Survivor Benefit Plan annuities are adjusted based on the Consumer Price Index. For last year's increase, DFAS said the COLA was effective December 1, 2025, and showed up in retirees' December 31 payment and SBP annuitants' early-January payment. Check DFAS and myPay for this year's details.

One exception: according to DFAS, retirees under the Career Status Bonus/REDUX system receive a COLA that is reduced by one percent. Their retired pay is recalculated at age 62, but later COLAs continue at the reduced rate.

## What 3.6% looks like in a real budget

A COLA is meant to keep your benefits from losing ground to prices. It is not really a raise. A few things to keep in mind:

- **Use the new numbers only once they arrive.** Wait for your notice or the official rate table before committing to a new expense.
- **Check what comes out first.** Medicare premiums, allotments, and debt offsets can all eat into the increase.
- **Look at what went up this year.** Rent, insurance, groceries, and utilities may have risen faster or slower than 3.6% for your household. Your own statements are a better guide than any average.
- **Decide where the difference goes before it hits your account.** Even a small increase can be directed to an emergency fund, a high-interest balance, or a bill that has been creeping up. If you do not choose, it tends to disappear into everyday spending.

If you live on a fixed income, the January payment is also when the holiday bills arrive. Planning for both at once can keep the new year from starting behind.

## The scams that follow every COLA

The Social Security Administration's Office of the Inspector General issued a warning in October: scammers are contacting people by phone, text, email, social media, and mail, claiming they can raise payments or "activate" the 2027 COLA.

According to the OIG, they may:

- Demand a fee
- Ask you to change your direct deposit
- Press you for personal or financial information
- Send official-looking links or documents, or tell you to download software

The COLA is automatic. The OIG says you do not need to sign up, apply, or take any action to receive it. The same goes for VA compensation increases and military retired pay COLAs — no one needs your bank login or a fee to "process" them.

If you get one of these contacts:

- **Hang up or do not reply.**
- **Look up the agency's number yourself** and call directly.
- **Report Social Security scams** at [ssa.gov/scam](https://www.ssa.gov/scam) or to the SSA OIG Fraud Hotline at 1-800-269-0271.
- **Report other impostor scams** to the FTC at [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/).

If someone claiming to be from VA asks you to change your direct deposit, do not do it through them. Sign in to VA.gov yourself, or call VA at a number you looked up.

## A note on this guide

COLA amounts and payment dates are set by each agency, and VA compensation rates depend on action by Congress. This is general information, not financial advice. Confirm your own amounts with SSA, VA, or DFAS.
