Veterans get targeted by credit repair offers. Some come by mail, some by phone, some through ads that mention military service specifically. A few of these companies are legitimate. Many are selling something you can do yourself for nothing.

Here is how to tell the difference.

## The thing no company can do

**Accurate negative information cannot be removed before it ages off.**

If you missed payments, and the report says you missed payments, that is accurate, and no company, attorney, or service can make it disappear. Negative information falls off on a schedule set by law. Until then it stays.

Any company promising to remove accurate negative items is either lying to you or planning to do something improper — and improper here can mean fraudulent, with consequences that land on you.

## What the law requires of them

The Credit Repair Organizations Act sets rules that legitimate companies follow. Under it, a credit repair organization:

- **Cannot charge you before services are performed.** Advance fees are prohibited.
- **Must give you a written contract** describing the services, the timeline, and the total cost.
- **Must tell you about your right to cancel** within three days, at no charge.
- **Cannot advise you to make false statements** to a credit bureau or lender.
- **Cannot tell you to create a "new identity"** — for example, by applying for an EIN to use in place of your Social Security number. This is fraud, and the person who signs the applications is you.

A company that violates any of these is telling you what it is.

## Warning signs

- Asks for payment upfront
- Guarantees a specific score increase, or guarantees removal of specific items
- Tells you not to contact the credit bureaus yourself
- Suggests disputing everything, including accurate items
- Will not give you a written contract before you pay
- Pressures you to decide immediately
- Mentions your military service to build trust quickly

That last one deserves attention. Being told "we specialize in helping veterans" is not evidence of anything. It is a sales approach, and it works because it sounds like solidarity.

## What you can do yourself, for free

Almost everything a credit repair company does, you can do at no cost:

**Get your reports.** Free weekly from AnnualCreditReport.com.

**Dispute errors.** Both credit bureaus and the businesses that furnished the information are required to investigate disputes. You file directly, free, and you can do it online or by mail.

**Add a statement to your file.** If a dispute is not resolved the way you think it should be, you can add a brief statement explaining your side.

**Negotiate with creditors.** You can contact a creditor or collection agency directly. Many will work with you, and no intermediary is required.

The work takes some time and attention. It does not take money.

## When paying someone makes sense

There are situations where professional help is reasonable — a complicated identity theft case, a mixed file that will not stay corrected, or a situation involving litigation. In those cases you may want an attorney rather than a credit repair company.

Nonprofit credit counseling is also a genuine category, and it is different from credit repair. Nonprofit counselors help with budgeting, debt management plans, and creditor negotiation, often free or low cost.
